### New Page: /resources/section-121-exclusion-us-expats/

**Target queries:** "section 121 exclusion expats," "home sale exclusion living abroad," "irs section 121 use test requirements"

**Angle:** US expats who've been living abroad often fail the 2-of-5-year use test without realizing there's a partial exclusion available. This is a common, costly mistake that international tax CPAs handle regularly.

**Must cover:**
- How the standard Section 121 exclusion works ($250k/$500k)
- The 2-of-5-year ownership and use test — how years abroad affect it
- The suspension rule: the 5-year window can be extended up to 10 years while on qualified official extended duty (military, foreign service) — does not apply to most civilians
- Partial exclusion: how to calculate the exclusion when you don't meet the full test
- The look-back rule: can't use the exclusion if you used it within the prior 2 years
- Non-qualified use: periods after 2008 where the home was not a primary residence reduce the exclusion proportionally
- Depreciation recapture: Section 121 does not exclude prior depreciation claimed on a home office or rental period
- Practical example: expat who lived abroad 3 of the last 5 years sells their US home — walk through the partial exclusion calculation
- What to do if the exclusion is partially or fully unavailable (installment sale, 1031 exchange considerations)
- Internal links to: /resources/capital-gains-home-sale-section-121/, /resources/physical-presence-test-us-expats/, /resources/feie-vs-ftc/

**Meta title:** Section 121 Home Sale Exclusion for US Expats: Partial Exclusion Rules | Vantis CPA
**Meta description:** US expats living abroad often fail the 2-of-5-year use test. Here's how to calculate the partial exclusion, how non-qualified use reduces it, and what to do when the full exclusion isn't available.

### New Page: /resources/section-121-exclusion-nonresident-aliens/

**Target queries:** "section 121 nonresident alien," "home sale exclusion foreign national," "non resident selling us property capital gains"

**Angle:** Non-residents generally cannot use Section 121 — but the rules around when they might qualify, and what alternatives exist, are genuinely complex. This is a question Vantis's foreign national clients ask constantly.

**Must cover:**
- Who qualifies as a nonresident alien for US tax purposes
- Can a nonresident alien use Section 121? (Generally no — must be a US resident for the use period)

- Partial-year residents: if you were a US resident for part of the 5-year window, does that qualify?
- Dual-status year: what happens in the year you become or stop being a US resident
- Treaty-based positions: do any US tax treaties affect the home sale exclusion for nonresidents?
- Planning opportunity: timing a home sale relative to residency status
- FIRPTA withholding on sale — the 15% withholding and how to reduce it with a withholding certificate

### New Page: /resources/form-3520/

**Target queries:** "form 3520 foreign gift," "form 3520 foreign inheritance," "irs form 3520 for expats," "3520 filing requirement"

**Must cover:**
- What Form 3520 is and who must file
- The three triggers: (1) receiving a gift or bequest from a foreign person above the threshold, (2) receiving a distribution from a foreign trust, (3) being a US grantor of a foreign trust
- 2025 reporting thresholds for foreign gifts vs foreign inheritance
- What counts as a "foreign person" — the definition matters
- Common situation: receiving an inheritance from a Latin American family member
- Common situation: receiving money from foreign parents to help buy a US home
- Penalties for failing to file: 25% of the amount received
- Late filing relief: reasonable cause exception, how to pursue it
- Interaction with Form 3520-A (foreign trust annual information return)
- Whether the gift or inheritance is taxable income (generally no — but it must be reported)
- Internal links to: /resources/foreign-gifts-and-inheritances-form-3520/, /services/international-tax/u-s-with-international-ties/

**Meta title:** Form 3520: Foreign Gifts, Inheritances, and Foreign Trust Reporting | Vantis CPA
**Meta description:** Form 3520 is required when US persons receive foreign gifts above the threshold, inherit from foreign persons, or have interests in foreign trusts. Here's who must file, the thresholds, and the penalties for missing it.
