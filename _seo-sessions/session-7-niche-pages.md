## Page 1 — /resources/accountable-plans-s-corp/

**Split from:** /resources/s-corp-owner-health-insurance-accountable-plans/ (which covers both topics and does neither fully)

**Target queries:** "accountable plan s corp," "s corp accountable plan," "s corp reimbursement plan," "accountable plan employee expenses"

**Angle:** S-corp owners who reimburse themselves for business expenses without a proper accountable plan are either leaving deductions on the table or creating payroll tax exposure. This page gives them the complete setup guide.

**Must cover:**
- What an accountable plan is and why S-corp owners need one
- The three IRS requirements for an accountable plan: business connection, substantiation, return of excess
- What expenses can be reimbursed through an accountable plan (vehicle, home office, phone, travel, meals, equipment)
- What cannot be reimbursed (personal expenses, commuting, clothing)
- How reimbursements avoid payroll tax — the core benefit
- Vehicle reimbursement: actual expense method vs IRS standard mileage rate through an accountable plan
- Home office reimbursement for S-corp owners who work from home (not deductible directly on 1120-S — must go through accountable plan)
- Health insurance for S-corp owners: why it's treated differently and does NOT go through an accountable plan
- How to document reimbursements: what records to keep, receipt requirements
- Common mistakes: lump-sum reimbursements without receipts, mixing personal and business on one credit card
- Internal links to: /resources/s-corp-owner-health-insurance-accountable-plans/, /resources/s-corp-election/, /resources/s-corp-reasonable-salary/

**Meta title:** Accountable Plans for S-Corp Owners: Reimbursing Business Expenses Tax-Free | Vantis CPA
**Meta description:** S-corp owners can reimburse themselves for business expenses tax-free through an accountable plan. Here's how to set one up, what qualifies, and what the IRS requires.

## Page 3 — /resources/pre-immigration-tax-planning/

**Expands:** /resources/pre-immigration-planning/ (which has 4 impressions at position 51 — needs more depth)

**Note:** Build this as a new, deeper page rather than editing the existing thin one. After it's built, redirect /resources/pre-immigration-planning/ to this new URL or replace the content in-place — your call based on the framework.

**Target queries:** "pre immigration tax planning," "us tax planning before moving to us," "pre immigration planning foreign nationals"

**Angle:** The window before someone becomes a US tax resident is one of the highest-value planning opportunities in international tax. Most people miss it entirely. This is a core Vantis service.

**Must cover:**
- When pre-immigration planning applies: the year before becoming a US resident (Green Card, substantial presence test)
- The mark-to-market election: electing to treat assets as sold before US residency begins, resetting cost basis
- Why this matters: gains that accrued while a nonresident are not subject to US tax — but only if you plan before the residency clock starts
- Foreign pension accounts: what to do with them before arriving (some countries allow tax-free lump sums only for non-residents)
- Foreign business interests: restructuring ownership before becoming a US person can avoid CFC (controlled foreign corporation) issues
- PFIC exposure: foreign mutual funds and investment funds become PFICs once you're a US person — what to do before that happens
- Estate planning: the US estate tax applies to worldwide assets of US domiciliaries — how residency affects this
- Social Security and totalization agreements: which countries have them and how they affect contributions
- Timing residency: when the substantial presence test kicks in and how to manage the first year
- Country-specific considerations: what varies by home country (mention Latin America, UK, Israel as examples)
- Internal links to: /resources/pfic-tax-rules/, /resources/form-8621/, /resources/fbar-fincen-114-who-must-file/, /services/international-tax/
