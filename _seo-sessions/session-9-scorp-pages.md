## Task 3 — Expand: /resources/ein-without-ssn-foreign-owners/

**Current status:** 48 impressions, position 44. This should be ranking page 1 — the topic is specific, Vantis's client base is exactly this, and the big tax sites don't cover it well. The page is probably too thin.

**Target queries:** "ein without ssn," "how to get ein without ssn," "ein for foreign owner," "itin vs ein foreign national"

**What to add:**
- Clear upfront answer: yes, foreign nationals can get an EIN without an SSN or ITIN
- The three methods in order of speed/ease:
  1. Phone (call IRS international line: 267-941-1099) — fastest, same day, requires a responsible party who can answer questions
  2. Fax (Form SS-4) — 4 business days
  3. Mail (Form SS-4) — 4 weeks
- Walk through the SS-4 form field by field — specifically the fields that trip up foreign applicants (responsible party, legal name, type of entity, reason for applying)
- Who counts as the "responsible party" — must be an individual, not an entity; the foreign owner is usually the responsible party
- What to say when the IRS asks why you need an EIN: "started a new business," "banking purposes," "compliance with IRS withholding requirements"
- EIN for a single-member foreign-owned LLC (a disregarded entity) — this is a very common situation
- After you get the EIN: Form 5472 filing requirement, opening a US bank account, getting an ITIN if needed separately
- Common mistake: applying online — online applications are not available for foreign applicants without an SSN
- Internal links to: /resources/smllc-5472/, /resources/itin-application-form-w-7/, /resources/foreign-owned-us-llc-ecommerce-sellers/

## Task 5 — Niche: /resources/s-corp-basis-and-ownership/

**Current status:** 26 impressions, position 73. Buried. The generic "S-corp basis" query is dominated by CPA exam study sites and major accounting blogs. Need a niche angle.

**Target queries:** "s corp shareholder basis," "s corp stock basis vs debt basis," "s corp basis for foreign shareholders"

**Rewrite angle:** "S-Corp Stock and Debt Basis: What Owners Need to Know to Deduct Losses"

The most useful angle is the practical one: owners want to know if they can deduct their S-corp losses, and the answer depends entirely on their basis. Lead with that.

**Must cover:**
- Why basis matters: you can only deduct S-corp losses to the extent of your basis in stock and debt
- Stock basis: starts at purchase price, increases with income and contributions, decreases with losses and distributions
- Debt basis: only the shareholder's own loans to the corporation — not third-party loans the shareholder guaranteed
- The ordering rules: basis is reduced in a specific order (distributions first, then losses)
- Suspended losses: what happens when losses exceed basis — they carry forward until basis is restored
- The at-risk rules and passive activity rules that further limit loss deductions beyond the basis rules
- Tracking basis year-by-year: why most owners don't do this and why it causes problems on exit
- Shareholder loan agreements: the requirements for a shareholder loan to create debt basis
- Foreign national as S-corp shareholder: S-corps cannot have nonresident alien shareholders — mention this clearly so readers who are foreign nationals know to stop and contact a CPA
- Internal links to: /resources/s-corp-election/, /resources/s-corp-vs-partnership/
