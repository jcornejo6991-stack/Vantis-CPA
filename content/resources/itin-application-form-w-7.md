An `ITIN`, an Individual Taxpayer Identification Number, exists for a specific group of people: those who have a reason to interact with the U.S. tax system but cannot get a Social Security number. A foreign spouse, a nonresident who owns a U.S. rental, an investor selling U.S. property, or a dependent abroad all need a U.S. tax ID to be on a return, and the ITIN is how they get one.

{{< pullquote attribution="What an ITIN does and does not do" >}}
An ITIN is purely a tax-processing number. It lets the IRS identify a filer. It does not grant the right to work, change anyone's immigration status, or open the door to Social Security benefits.
{{< /pullquote >}}

## Who Actually Needs One

You apply for an ITIN with `Form W-7`. The common situations:

- A **foreign spouse** of a U.S. citizen or resident who wants to file a joint return
- **Dependents** who are foreign nationals being claimed on a U.S. return
- A **foreign owner of a U.S. LLC or business** who must file Form 1040-NR (see [foreign-owned U.S. LLCs](foreign-owned-us-llc-ecommerce-sellers.md))
- A **foreign seller of U.S. real estate** subject to [FIRPTA withholding](firpta-us-real-estate-sales.md), who needs an ITIN to file and claim a refund
- A **nonresident claiming a tax treaty benefit** that reduces U.S. withholding
- A nonresident with **U.S.-source income** subject to reporting

The unifying theme: a U.S. tax filing or reporting obligation, paired with ineligibility for an SSN. If a person can get a Social Security number, they should, an ITIN is not an alternative.

## How the Application Works

In most cases, Form W-7 is **not** filed on its own. It is attached to the **federal tax return** that creates the need for the number, and the two are processed together. (Certain exceptions, such as treaty-based withholding or third-party reporting, let you apply without a return.)

Every application requires proof of two things:

- **Identity**
- **Foreign status**

A valid, unexpired **passport** is the one document that proves both on its own. Without a passport, you must provide a combination of other documents (national ID, birth certificate, visa, and so on) from the IRS-approved list.

## The Document Problem, and How to Avoid It

Here is the practical sticking point. The IRS requires **original documents or copies certified by the issuing agency**, and the default method is to **mail them in**. For most people, that means sending an original passport to the IRS and waiting weeks to get it back.

{{< callout title="Use a Certifying Acceptance Agent" tone="opportunity" >}}
A Certifying Acceptance Agent (CAA) is authorized by the IRS to verify your identity documents in person and certify them on your behalf. You bring your passport, the CAA confirms it, and you keep it. This is the single best reason to use a professional for an ITIN: you never have to mail your passport across borders or be without it during processing.
{{< /callout >}}

Your three options:

| Method | Mail original passport? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Certifying Acceptance Agent (CAA) | No | Passport verified in person and certified |
| IRS Taxpayer Assistance Center | No | By appointment; limited locations |
| Mail to the IRS | Yes (or agency-certified copy) | Simplest to start, but you part with your document |

## Timing and Expiration

Processing typically takes around **7 to 11 weeks**, and longer during the busy filing season from January through April. Because the ITIN is usually tied to a return, a delayed ITIN means a delayed return, so applying early matters when a deadline is involved.

{{< callout title="ITINs expire from disuse" tone="warning" >}}
An ITIN that goes unused on a federal return for **three consecutive years** expires. If you have an older ITIN and are about to file after a gap, check whether it needs renewal first, filing with an expired ITIN causes processing delays and can hold up credits and refunds. Renewal uses the same Form W-7.
{{< /callout >}}

## When to Seek Help

A simple ITIN application filed alongside a clean return is manageable on your own, if you are willing to mail your passport. The case for professional help is strongest when you **do not want to part with your passport** (a CAA solves this), when the ITIN supports a more complex filing like a [FIRPTA refund](firpta-us-real-estate-sales.md) or a treaty claim, or when **multiple family members** need numbers at once. Getting the documents and the underlying return right the first time is what prevents the most common outcome of a do-it-yourself ITIN: a rejection notice weeks later, with the filing deadline now much closer.
