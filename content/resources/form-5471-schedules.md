Form 5471 is best understood not as a form but as a filing system: a few pages of identifying information followed by a stack of schedules, each reporting a different slice of the foreign corporation's life. Which slices you owe depends on your [filer category](form-5471-categories-of-filers.md). This guide walks the schedules in order, says what each one actually reports, and flags where the preparation time really goes. For the form's overall triggers and penalties, start with the [Form 5471 overview](form-5471.md).

{{< pullquote attribution="The preparation reality" >}}
Nobody struggles with Form 5471 because the boxes are confusing. They struggle because the boxes ask for U.S.-style financial statements and an earnings history that the foreign company was never required to produce for anyone.
{{< /pullquote >}}

## The Schedules on the Main Form

**Schedule A: stock of the foreign corporation.** Each class of stock and the shares outstanding at the start and end of the year.

**Schedule B: shareholders.** Part I lists the U.S. shareholders owning 10% or more; Part II lists direct owners. This is where the ownership chart you built for the category analysis gets memorialized.

**Schedule C: income statement.** The corporation's profit and loss for the year, presented in functional currency and U.S. dollars, following U.S. accounting concepts. Local statutory accounts rarely map cleanly; a conversion workpaper bridging local GAAP to U.S. presentation is standard.

**Schedule F: balance sheet.** Assets, liabilities, and equity in U.S. dollars, again on U.S. concepts. Together with Schedule C this is the "produce financial statements" core of the form.

**Schedule G: other information.** A long and growing list of yes/no questions covering related-party dealings, foreign tax matters, and elections, several of which trigger additional attachments (Schedule G-1) or cross-reference other reporting regimes. Answering Schedule G carelessly is a classic way to contradict the rest of the return.

## The Separate Schedules

These file as standalone attachments, and they are where CFC reporting gets heavy:

**Schedule E (and E-1): income taxes paid or accrued.** Every foreign income tax the corporation paid, allocated across income categories. Schedule E is the feeder for [foreign tax credit](form-1116-foreign-tax-credit.md) computations and for the deemed-paid credits available to corporate shareholders and Section 962 electors.

**Schedule H: current earnings and profits.** The year's profit restated under U.S. earnings and profits (E&P) principles, which differ from both local books and U.S. taxable income. E&P is the currency in which all CFC consequences are denominated.

**Schedule I (and I-1): the shareholder's inclusions.** Schedule I summarizes what the U.S. shareholder must pick up in income: Subpart F, investments in U.S. property, and related items. Schedule I-1 reports the tested income data that drives the [GILTI / net CFC tested income calculation](gilti-tax.md). These schedules are where the information return starts writing numbers onto your actual tax return.

**Schedule J: accumulated E&P.** The corporation's E&P history sorted into categories, including the pools of **previously taxed earnings and profits (PTEP)** created by past Subpart F and GILTI inclusions. Schedule J is what lets a later distribution come out tax-free instead of being taxed twice, which makes it one of the most valuable schedules to maintain accurately, and the single hardest one to reconstruct for a company that has existed for decades.

**Schedule M: related-party transactions.** Every flow between the CFC and its U.S. shareholders and related entities: sales, services, rents, royalties, loans, interest, dividends. Schedule M is examination bait; it is the schedule the IRS reads to find transfer pricing issues and disguised distributions, the same role Form 5472 plays for [foreign-owned U.S. companies](smllc-5472.md) in the other direction.

**Schedule O: organization, acquisitions, and dispositions.** The event schedule for Category 2 and 3 filers: who acquired or disposed of what, when, and for how much. In the year a company is formed, acquired, or reorganized, Schedule O carries the story.

**Schedule P: previously taxed E&P of the shareholder.** The shareholder-level mirror of Schedule J, tracking each U.S. shareholder's own PTEP accounts. J and P have to reconcile, year over year, shareholder by shareholder.

**Schedule Q: income by CFC income groups.** Tested income and other income sorted into the groupings the foreign tax credit rules require. Schedule Q exists so that credits land in the right basket, and it is the schedule most likely to be blank on a self-prepared form and most conspicuous to a reviewer when it is.

**Schedule R: distributions.** What the corporation actually paid out during the year, which, read against Schedules J and P, determines how much of a distribution is PTEP (tax-free), dividend, or basis recovery.

## Which Categories File Which Schedules

The IRS instructions carry the authoritative chart, but the shape is consistent:

| Filer | Typical schedule load |
|---|---|
| Category 2 | Identifying information and Schedule O (Part I) |
| Category 3 | Identifying information, Schedule O (Part II), plus ownership and financial detail for the year of change |
| Category 4 | The full financial picture: Schedules A, B, C, F, G, plus E, H, M and related attachments |
| Category 5 | Everything the CFC regime needs: the Category 4 set plus Schedules I, I-1, J, P, Q, and R |
| Category 1 | A reduced version of the Category 5 set, depending on subcategory |

Unrelated and constructive-only subcategories (1b, 1c, 5b, 5c) shave schedules off these lists, which is exactly why the [category analysis](form-5471-categories-of-filers.md) is worth doing carefully before anyone opens the software.

## Where the Time Actually Goes

Three tasks consume most Form 5471 engagements. **Translation:** local books have to be restated in U.S. dollars under the functional-currency rules, on U.S. accounting concepts. **E&P work:** current and accumulated earnings and profits must be computed under U.S. principles, and for a company with history, reconstructed from inception, because Schedules J and P are cumulative. **Reconciliation:** Schedules C, F, H, J, P, and R all have to tie to each other and to last year's filing, and examiners check exactly that.

The efficient version of this is a system, not an annual scramble: a chart of accounts mapping, a translation workpaper that rolls forward, and E&P and PTEP ledgers maintained every year. Set up once, the incremental annual cost drops sharply.

{{< callout title="Incomplete is unfiled" tone="warning" >}}
A Form 5471 that is substantially incomplete, with required schedules blank or estimated, can be treated as never filed. That means the [$10,000 penalty](form-5471-penalties.md) and an open statute of limitations on the entire return, even though something was attached. If a number cannot be produced by the deadline, the answer is an extension and a proper filing, not a placeholder.
{{< /callout >}}
